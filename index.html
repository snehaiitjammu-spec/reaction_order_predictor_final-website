2. Method Selection — What is the difference?

Integrated (integral) method

We integrate the rate law and transform concentration into a quantity that should vary linearly with time if the assumed order is correct.

n = 0: CA = CA0 − kt
n = 1: ln CA = ln CA0 − kt
n ≠ 1: CA1−n = CA01−n + (n−1)kt

Why use it? It uses the entire concentration–time dataset directly and is generally less sensitive to experimental noise than numerical differentiation.

Differential method

We estimate the instantaneous rate from the concentration–time data and compare rate with concentration.

−dCA/dt = kCAn
ln(rate) = ln k + n ln CA

Why use it? It estimates the order directly from the rate law and is a useful independent check, but numerical differentiation can amplify measurement noise.
